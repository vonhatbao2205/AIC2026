\begin{abstract}
Interactive video search connects incomplete descriptions and evolving hints
to verifiable moments. We present ClueScope, a multimodal retrieval system
for Video Browser Showdown 2027 organized around inspectable evidence.
Its retrieval pipeline combines complementary visual encoders with scene text,
speech, and audio cues through rank-based fusion, while an optional multi-scale
clip branch contributes temporal context at video level. Ranked video groups
retain supporting frames, timestamps, and channel provenance for verification.
For descriptions revealed over successive turns, Progressive Hint Memory (PHM)
maintains an editable hint ledger and persistent video-level evidence.
Bounded in-video searches revisit surviving candidates and check newly retrieved
videos against earlier hints; a similarity-frontier rule distinguishes evidence
eligible for ranking from matches retained only for localization. A unified
workspace integrates spatial query composition, chronological browsing,
source-video playback, visual question answering assistance, and ad-hoc result
collection with operator-reviewed DRES submission. A controlled component study
uses 60 held-out synthetic progressive queries over 1.34 million keyframes from
873 collected videos. With two visual encoders, PHM achieves a mean-prefix video
MRR of 0.6903, compared with 0.6662 for independent-hint rank fusion and 0.6907
for cumulative retrieval, at a median retrieval latency of 3.88 seconds.
These results characterize the cost of explicit evidence memory without
establishing a ranking advantage over cumulative search.
\keywords{Interactive video retrieval \and Multimodal search \and Progressive hints \and Evidence inspection}
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
Interactive video retrieval connects partial scene descriptions to precise
moments. Appearance, scene text, speech, sound, and multi-frame events call for
complementary retrieval channels. Operators must then establish which clues
support each candidate and where to verify them. With successive hints, useful
evidence must remain inspectable as the description changes. Incremental
descriptions occur in textual and conversational VBS tasks~\cite{vbs2025results}.

Video Browser Showdown (VBS) 2027 emphasizes textual and chat-based known-item
search (KIS-T/KIS-C), visual question answering (VQA), and ad-hoc video search
(AVS), alongside visual KIS~\cite{vbs2027}. ClueScope
connects search clues to operator-selected answers through video-grouped
evidence, temporal context, progressive search, and answer review. Its contributions are threefold:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Multimodal evidence integration.} A retrieval architecture fuses
visual, OCR, speech, and audio rankings and optionally incorporates multi-scale
clips at video level, retaining timestamps and channel provenance for inspection.
\item \textbf{Inspectable progressive search.} PHM links an editable hint ledger
to persistent candidate evidence, with bounded rescue and backfill searches and
an explicit rule governing the ranking eligibility of local matches.
\item \textbf{An integrated verification workflow.} Spatial queries, contextual
playback, VQA assistance, and an AVS basket connect candidate discovery to
operator-controlled answer review and DRES submission.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related Work}
Fusionista2.0 and U-Cker emphasize query assistance~\cite{fusionista2026,ucker2026}, while VIREO and PraK support
spatial constraints and contextual browsing~\cite{vireo2026,prak2026}.
SnapMind exposes editable retrieval plans and source attribution~\cite{snapmind2026};
NII-UIT couples answer suggestions with in-video evidence discovery~\cite{nii2026}.
Exquisitor integrates conversational interaction, feedback, and in-video
search~\cite{exquisitor2026,exquisitor2025interactive}. H-EAGLE organizes representations at multiple
semantic levels~\cite{heagle2026}, and VERGE provides collection-specific
modalities~\cite{verge2026}. vitrivr supports emotion retrieval; TapesVRy and MediaMix offer immersive
exploration~\cite{vitrivr2026,tapesvry2026,mediamix2026}.
ClueScope emphasizes persistent evidence across released hints, exposing the
provenance and ranking eligibility of local matches alongside contextual browsing.

\section{Multimodal Retrieval Architecture}
\label{sec:architecture}
\begin{figure}[htbp]
\centering
\resizebox{\textwidth}{!}{\input{figures/architecture.tex}}
\caption{ClueScope: offline preparation and interactive retrieval. TARA joins at video
level; PHM uses pre-TARA evidence. Dashed arrows indicate local searches and refinement.}
\label{fig:architecture}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{Indexed evidence.}
The collection contains 873 videos and 1,339,055 keyframes
(Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture}), sampled using InfoShot-style sampling~\cite{infoshot2026}
extended for short events and text changes. A registry maps keyframes to source frames and playback times.
PE-Core-G14-448~\cite{pe2025} provides normalized 1,280-dimensional embeddings;
Qwen3-VL-Embedding-8B~\cite{qwen2026} supplies a separate 4,096-dimensional space.
Both are searched in Milvus~\cite{milvus2021}. Elasticsearch holds metadata, OCR, speech, audio, and object annotations.
FastAPI coordinates retrieval; React/TypeScript provides the console.

\paragraph{Query interpretation and fusion.}
Operators select models, channels, depth, and index-level scope filters.
Heuristics or an optional language model extract channel-specific cues, with
translation and optional expansion for visual descriptions; OCR and speech
preserve original-language cues. Parallel retrieval combines
visual search with exact/numeric and fuzzy OCR matching, confidence-aware speech
ranking, and audio tags, captions, or embeddings.

For frame $f$, weighted reciprocal rank fusion (RRF)~\cite{rrf2009} computes
\begin{equation}
 R(f)=\sum_{c:f\in L_c}\frac{w_c}{60+r_c(f)},\qquad r_c(f)\geq1,
 \label{eq:rrf}
\end{equation}
where $L_c$ is a channel ranking. Optional Qwen reranking merges a deduplicated visual pool into one ranking;
otherwise PE and Qwen contribute separate rankings. Raw scores across embedding
spaces are not added. Channel attribution, supporting text, timestamps, and availability warnings
remain visible.

\paragraph{Group-by-video aggregation and clip context.}
Video groups retain chronological frames and channel provenance. Videos are ranked by their strongest frame (weight 0.75),
mean top-three score (0.15), and bounded temporal support (0.10). Frame scores
are normalized by the global maximum; support saturates at four additional
matches within 30 seconds of the strongest frame. Separated temporal clusters
are flagged for inspection.

Optional TARA retrieval~\cite{tara2026} indexes 168,536 clips over 8-, 24-, and
72-second windows from the same videos. Each scale contributes at most one vote per video. RRF combines scale rankings,
then fuses them with frame-channel video rankings. Clip intervals support
playback, including for videos absent from frame results.

\section{Interactive Search and Evidence Verification}
\label{sec:interaction}
\begin{figure}[!htbp]
\centering
\includegraphics[width=.94\textwidth]{console-en.png}
\caption{The console: query controls (left), video groups (center), and frame evidence
(right). Candidates and scores are illustrative; source images are real.}
\label{fig:console}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{From a description to a verified moment.}
Video groups expose chronological frames, strongest matches, and channel
evidence (Fig.~\ref{fig:console}). Operators reject or prioritize videos, search by
frame similarity, and inspect playback and registry neighbors beyond retrieved
hits. Timeline seeking preserves source coordinates; tabs and notes retain hypotheses.

\paragraph{Visual KIS via spatial queries.}
After observing a KIS-V clip~\cite{vbs2027}, the operator reconstructs objects,
colors, positions, and actions on a canvas. One-to-one Hungarian assignment~\cite{kuhn1955}
scores Elasticsearch object detections against query objects. RRF combines
this ranking with PE searches using generated descriptions and an optional
low-weight rasterized sketch. The operator verifies matches through playback.

\paragraph{Evidence-supported VQA.}
NVILA~\cite{nvila2025} suggests answers with supporting frame identifiers.
Our optional adapter uses DeepSeek-V4.1-Flash~\cite{deepseek2026} for external
knowledge, subject to provider tool support. ClueScope separately verifies
source-linked alternatives with NVILA, rejecting visual contradictions;
web confidence cannot override visual evidence. Operators review suggestions
and uncalibrated scores. Web grounding remains separate from indexed retrieval.

\paragraph{AVS: collecting relevant moments.}
An \emph{anchors-only} queue applies two-second temporal non-maximum suppression
within each video. Successive passes propose one anchor per video in relevance
order, prioritizing videos with fewer accepted moments. No KIS temporal offsets
are added. Operators verify playback and accept moments into a task-scoped basket
that survives query refinement. Exact duplicates are blocked; nearby moments
require an explicit override. Acceptance is separate from submission and a DRES verdict.

\paragraph{Answer review and DRES submission.}
A question-specific table supports frame editing, duplicate checks, CSV export,
and optional operator synchronization. When configured, the DRES~\cite{dres2024} v2 adapter
supports login, task/hint discovery, millisecond coordinates, preview, and verdict
history. Operators can enable direct submission and confirm individual answers
or accepted AVS moments. A changed task or unavailable connection blocks sending.
Mock-transport tests cover AVS routing, temporal payloads, and duplicate protection;
live VBS validation remains pending.

\section{Progressive Hint Memory}
\label{sec:phm}
PHM maintains an editable ledger of released hints (Fig.~\ref{fig:phm}). Exact duplicates add no vote; cumulative appends contribute their new suffix,
and edits invalidate superseded evidence. Only released, enabled hints participate.

\centering
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.33\textwidth}\vspace{0pt}
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{progressive-ledger.png}
\end{minipage}\hspace{.02\textwidth}
\begin{minipage}[t]{0.52\textwidth}\vspace{0pt}
\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{progressive-evidence.png}
\end{minipage}
\caption{PHM: released hints (left); candidate history, evidence origins, and frames
(right). This archived PE-only session predates the benchmark's frontier revision.}
\label{fig:phm}
\end{figure}

After per-hint fusion and grouping, video rank $r_i(v)$ gives evidence
$e_i(v)=61/(60+r_i(v))$. Unobserved videos score zero but retain an explicit
unobserved label: truncated retrieval does not establish irrelevance.
For hints $I_t$ with a valid channel observation and current cumulative rank
evidence $C_t(v)$, we compute
\begin{equation}
 M_t(v)=\left[\prod_{i\in I_t}\bigl(0.10+0.90e_i(v)\bigr)\right]^{1/|I_t|},
 \qquad S_t(v)=0.70M_t(v)+0.30C_t(v).
 \label{eq:phm}
\end{equation}
These presets are not probabilities; historical cumulative queries are not
independent hints.

Subsequent turns search up to eight survivors lacking new-hint evidence and
backfill older hints for up to four newcomers, capped at 16 video--hint pairs
and three frames per model/pair. Query vectors are session-cached. Local hits enter
ranking only if their raw similarity reaches the lowest finite similarity in
the original global list for the \emph{same model, query, and collection}.
Eligible hits are deduplicated and re-ranked with that list before fusion.
These candidate-pool ranks are not exact corpus ranks. Hits below an observed
frontier, or without one, remain localization-only evidence.

Memory retains at most 250 videos, protecting current and previous top-20
candidates, with three temporal representatives per hint/video. The UI exposes
hint support, rank history, and global/rescue/backfill origins. Revision checks
reject stale responses; process-local sessions expire after inactivity and support
journal replay. PHM disables expansion, reranking, and TARA; optional
OCR/speech/audio channels are outside the visual-only evaluation.

\section{Controlled Component Evaluation}
\label{sec:evaluation}
We derive 75 owner-approved synthetic progressive queries from existing
descriptions and sampled video inspection. Target-video connected components
separate 15 development and 60 held-out queries (55 groups), each with three
prefixes. Approval does not establish independent continuous-playback verification.
Both PE and PE+Qwen search all 1,339,055 indexed frames at depth 200 per model/view.
Code (\texttt{b012b9b}), annotations, translations, parser plans, and settings
were frozen; auxiliary modalities, TARA, expansion, and reranking are disabled.

Baselines use the full released description (cumulative), independent hints
(Hint-RRF), or PHM without local searches. Video MRR, capped at 250 videos, is
averaged over prefixes then queries. All 1,800 held-out method prefixes passed
prefix-isolation and metric audits.

PHM shows no demonstrated MRR advantage over cumulative search
(Table~\ref{tab:results}): differences are $-0.0099$ for PE (95\% interval
$[-0.0576,0.0358]$) and $-0.0004$ for PE+Qwen ($[-0.0460,0.0431]$).
Equivalence is not established. Differences from Hint-RRF are $0.0367$ and
$0.0241$, with positive unadjusted intervals from 10,000 paired
video-group bootstrap resamples~\cite{efron1979}; comparisons remain exploratory.
Rescue can worsen individual prefixes. A depth-400 cumulative control reached
0.6627 MRR at 0.53\,s median latency on PE; depth was selected using development
latency, without achieving a compute match to PHM.
Latency excludes translation preparation and trace serialization and varies
with service load. Operator effort and AVS effectiveness remain unmeasured.
PHM provides explicit historical evidence at additional cost.

\section{Deployment and Conclusion}
ClueScope connects multimodal retrieval and progressive evidence to operator
verification, VQA, AVS collection, and answer submission. VBS deployment requires
ingestion and identity/time mappings for V3C~\cite{v3c2019}, Marine Video
Kit~\cite{mvk2023}, and GynSurg~\cite{gynsurg2025},
live-server validation, and interaction logs. Evaluating operator effort remains
a next step beyond the controlled retrieval study.
